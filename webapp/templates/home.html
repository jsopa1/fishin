What this is — and isn't

This is NOT a validated catch-rate prediction tool. It does not tell you whether you will catch a fish, how many fish are in a given waterbody, or estimate a "fishing score" of any kind. Every statement it generates is a factual comparison — is the current water temperature inside a documented physiological window for a species known to be present here — never a forecast of angler success.

This distinction is deliberate and load-bearing. An earlier phase of this project (V0) tested whether real catch-rate data from Wisconsin lakes could be statistically predicted from weather, water conditions, and fish physiology. The honest answer, after several independent research cycles, was no — not with the public data available today. Rather than force a predictive claim the evidence doesn't support, this tool reports a different, honestly-scoped kind of information: real conditions, compared against real biology, for a real, sourced list of species — nothing more.

Every prediction record shown here also honestly discloses its evidentiary tier: whether a species' presence at a waterbody is survey-confirmed (an actual WDNR fisheries survey observed it there) or only stocking-only (WDNR has stocked it there, which is positive evidence but not proof of a complete species list — a species can be genuinely present without ever having been stocked). Temperature readings are labeled real when they come from an actual sensor, estimated when interpolated from nearby real readings, or proxy when they're an approximate regional air-temperature stand-in. Where sources genuinely disagree on a physiological threshold (for example, Muskellunge's documented thermal optimum), that disagreement is shown in full, not resolved to a single convenient number.

Full technical documentation, source data, and every research report behind this tool are public in the project repository: github.com/jsopa1/fishin.
